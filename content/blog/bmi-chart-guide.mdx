Body Mass Index (BMI) remains one of the most widely used screening metrics across clinical medicine and public health epidemiology. Created in the 19th century by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet, BMI provides an accessible, non-invasive method for evaluating whether an individual's weight is proportional to their stature.

## The World Health Organization (WHO) BMI Categories

The standard classification system categorizes adult BMI values into four primary tiers:

| BMI Range | Classification | Clinical Implications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **< 18.5** | Underweight | Potential nutritional deficiencies and compromised immunity |
| **18.5 – 24.9** | Normal Weight | Optimal metabolic profile and lowest chronic disease risk |
| **25.0 – 29.9** | Overweight | Mildly elevated risk of hypertension and cardiovascular strain |
| **30.0 and above** | Obese | Increased risk for Type 2 diabetes, sleep apnea, and joint stress |

## How Is BMI Calculated?

BMI uses a simple metric formula:

$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight (kg)}}{[\text{Height (m)}]^2}$$

For imperial units (pounds and inches), the formula includes a conversion scalar:

$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight (lbs)} \times 703}{[\text{Height (inches)}]^2}$$

## Key Limitations of BMI

While BMI is exceptionally valuable for population-level screening, it has acknowledged diagnostic limitations for individual athletes:
- **Muscle Mass vs. Fat**: Muscle tissue is much denser than adipose tissue. Bodybuilders and strength athletes often score in the "overweight" or "obese" ranges despite single-digit body fat percentages.
- **Fat Distribution**: BMI does not distinguish between visceral abdominal fat (which poses cardiovascular risks) and subcutaneous fat on the hips and thighs.
- **Bone Density and Age**: Senior adults naturally experience sarcopenia (muscle loss), which means an elderly person might have a "normal" BMI while carrying excess visceral adipose tissue.

## Complementary Health Measurements

To gain a holistic view of your physical health, pair your BMI evaluation with:
1. **Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR)**: Measures central fat deposition.
2. **Body Composition Analysis**: DEXA scans or bioelectrical impedance analysis.
3. **Blood Biomarkers**: Lipid panels, HbA1c, and fasting glucose levels.
